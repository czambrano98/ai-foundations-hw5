Reflection

1. How would I evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

I graded the team on three things: did it get the shop facts right, did it respect the money rules, and did it route the work efficiently. On facts and money rules it went three for three. Efficiency is where the tickets differed.

Ticket 101 (Bulldog tee): solid, a little over-delegated. The team found the real blocker instead of the obvious one. Size S was 0 on hand, but the thing actually stopping the order was the open $840 invoice #501, which makes Bulldog Print Co's can_ship false (the 101 tab shows get_inventory, list_open_invoices, and get_vendor all got used). It drafted the vendor order and the customer reply and then stopped for approval instead of paying, which is exactly what I wanted. The ding: the Boss delegated to Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service all at once, so Accounting got pulled in twice and the ticket got read more than once. Right answer, some wasted motion.

Ticket 102 (Rent): the cleanest of the three. Boss to Facilities to Accounting, the exact minimal chain (the 102 tab shows only 3 steps). Facilities confirmed lease #1 at $2,400 due 2026-09-02, Accounting confirmed the $2,560 cushion, and it stopped for approval. No wasted motion and a correct payment.

Ticket 103 (Bulk discount): good judgment and good restraint. Customer Service treated this as a pricing decision, not a payment. It pulled margin from get_pricing (cost $22, list $58), proposed 10% off at $52.20 a unit which still keeps $30.20 of margin, and flagged the 12-unit shortfall (8 of 20 in stock). No cash moved, which the Cash tab confirms as a $0.00 change. The weak spot: it could not confirm a restock vendor or an ETA, so it left fulfillment open and only drafted a reply instead of a purchase order.

2. For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan we wrote?

101: We expected Accounting first, then Accounting to Inventory, using get_inventory, list_open_invoices, and pay_invoice. The destination matched (pay invoice #501) but the path was wider. The Boss went to all three specialists at once, and the invoice path actually ran Inventory to Accounting. The team also used more tools than we predicted (get_vendor, get_cash_balance, draft_vendor_order, draft_customer_reply). And pay_invoice never ran inside the agent loop; it ran at my approval, which is how we built it.

102: Near-perfect match. We predicted Boss to Facilities to Accounting with get_lease and pay_rent, and that is exactly what happened. The only extras were sensible confirmations (get_cash_balance, get_desk_date, update_ticket), and pay_rent again ran at approval, not in the loop.

103: The people and the tools matched the plan (Customer Service first, then Inventory and Accounting, using get_pricing and get_inventory). The one divergence went in our favor: we expected it to also run list_open_invoices and pay_invoice for the restock, but invoice #501 was already paid during ticket 101, so the team correctly did not re-pay. Our plan slightly over-predicted work that an earlier ticket had already cleared.

The pattern: across all three, the agents were more parallel than our linear plans, and in every case the payment landed at the human approval step, never inside the run.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102 (rent) would have been simpler as a single agent with tools. It is a linear, single-owner task: read lease #1, check cash, stop for approval. There is no real cross-specialty question in it, yet it went through two delegation hops (Boss to Facilities to Accounting). One agent with get_lease, get_cash_balance, and the approval step could have done the whole thing with fewer tokens and no handoffs, and the 102 tab shows that is literally all the tools it touched plus update_ticket.

Ticket 101's happy path is similar once the diagnosis is made: "pay the blocking invoice #501" is a single action, and our full-connectivity design actually hurt us there by letting the Boss over-delegate to all three specialists. The multi-agent setup only earns its keep on ticket 103, where the decision genuinely spans three areas at once: the margin (pricing), the stock shortfall (inventory), and whether a restock can even ship (vendor). So the rote, single-owner tickets are overkill for a team; the real teamwork value shows up only when one decision crosses specialties.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this team could solve with the tools we built.

All three reuse the exact agents and tools we already have.

a) A second blocking invoice with no ticket attached. Inventory wants to restock a dead SKU (our data has CC-MUG-CREST at 0 on hand and CC-TEE-WHITE size S at 0), and the chosen vendor has an open bill. This is ticket 101's pattern with a different vendor: Accounting runs list_open_invoices and get_cash_balance, flags it, and pay_invoice clears it after approval so get_vendor's can_ship flips to true.

b) Rent coming due on a second space. If Campus Customs signs another lease, Facilities watches next_due with get_lease and get_desk_date and routes to Accounting for pay_rent after approval. This is ticket 102 exactly, and our pay_rent already advances next_due to the next month (it moved lease #1 from 2026-09-02 to 2026-10-02 in this run).

c) A restock-and-quote for a low-stock item. A customer asks for a SKU we are short on (like the 12-unit hoodie gap in ticket 103, or the 0-stock mug). Inventory checks get_inventory, picks a supplier by specialty with get_vendor (Elm City Gifts for mugs at a 3-day lead, Bulldog Print Co for apparel at a 5-day lead), drafts the purchase order with draft_vendor_order, and Customer Service drafts the reply with the ETA using draft_customer_reply. That is tickets 101 and 103 combined, with no new tools.

5. Three new problems this team could NOT solve with the tools we built, and what each would need.

a) Actually fulfilling a paid order. After I approved invoice #501, the reprint still has to be produced, arrive, and ship to Tauhid. We only have draft_vendor_order (draft, placed:false) and no way to place a real order, receive stock, or create a shipment. The 101 tab ends with "draft PO on the board, not sent," and inventory.qty never increases. This needs a place_vendor_order tool, a receive_stock tool that increments inventory, and a fulfill_order / create_shipment tool, owned by Inventory or a new Logistics agent.

b) Billing the customer and bringing cash in. Our whole money model is one-directional: cash only goes out, and get_cash_balance with pay_invoice / pay_rent only subtract. The 103 bulk order is 20 hoodies at $52.20, which is $1,044 of revenue the shop cannot record. We would need an issue_customer_invoice tool and a record_payment_received tool that credits cash_accounts, plus lifting the no-income rule, owned by Accounting. The Cash tab makes the gap obvious: it only ever shows outflows.

c) Actually sending anything to a customer or vendor. By design draft_customer_reply and draft_vendor_order never send or place, so every run ends with drafts left on the board. If the Yale AI Club discount reply or Tauhid's reprint ETA genuinely needs to go out, we cannot send it. This needs a send_customer_message tool and a real send/place tool, both gated behind human approval the same way payments are, owned by Customer Service and Inventory. All three of our runs ended with unsent drafts, so this gap shows up on every ticket.
